Opposition to proposed data centerFrom a verified resident via globaldatacenters.net · To the Stafford County board

Dear Stafford County Board of Supervisors,

My name is Rosanne B., and I live in Stafford County. I know this board faces constant pressure to say yes to data centers given how much of northern Virginia's economy now runs on them, but I'm writing to ask you to slow down on this one.

Northern Virginia already has a documented history of what happens when localities approve facility after facility without a real coordinated plan: overloaded substations, rate pressure creeping onto residential bills, and communities that lose their character piece by piece as more land gets converted to industrial use. I don't want Stafford to be the next cautionary example.

I'm asking for an independent hydrological study before any permit moves forward, one that names the actual water source, the projected daily draw, and what that means for residential wells and the Rappahannock watershed. On the grid, Dominion Energy's transmission system in this region is already strained from the concentration of data centers nearby, and I want a public capacity study before any new interconnection is approved, along with a guarantee that residential ratepayers won't be subsidizing infrastructure built for this one company. On noise and property values, this county's neighborhoods weren't planned around industrial infrastructure, and any approval needs binding noise limits and a real look at value impacts on nearby homes.

Relevant statutes: Code of Virginia § 15.2-2280 (zoning ordinance authority) and Code of Virginia § 62.1-44.15 (State Water Control Law).

Please require full independent studies and public disclosure before any action.

Respectfully,
Rosanne B.
Stafford County, Virginia

D
Reply from Deuntay T. DiggsChairman, Stafford County Board of Supervisors

Dear Ms. B.,

Thank you for taking the time to share your concerns. Many of the issues you identified, water usage, electrical infrastructure, noise, impacts on surrounding properties, and appropriate siting, are exactly why Stafford County has taken a much more restrictive approach to data centers than many other Northern Virginia localities.

I also want to emphasize that Stafford has the benefit of learning from what has occurred elsewhere in Northern Virginia. We do not have to repeat the mistakes of jurisdictions that experienced rapid data center development before fully understanding the cumulative impacts.

That is why I have consistently supported strong setbacks, noise protections, careful consideration of water resources and infrastructure, and locating these facilities only where they are compatible with surrounding communities. I believe economic development and protection of our residents can and must occur together.

I appreciate your specific requests regarding hydrology, grid capacity, residential rate impacts, noise, and property values. I will make sure these concerns remain part of the discussion as proposals are evaluated.

My goal is not to make Stafford the next Loudoun or Prince William County. It is to learn from their experiences and determine whether Stafford can capture appropriate commercial investment while protecting the character, resources, and residents of our county.

Thank you again for engaging with the Board and for providing thoughtful, specific concerns for us to consider.

Respectfully,
Deuntay T. Diggs, MPA
Stafford County Chairman
George Washington District

What your commissioners receive.

A letter from a verified, real constituent

Addressed from your name and street address. Commissioners can verify you're a real resident in their district. Not anonymous — real opposition on the record.

Your concerns backed by law

Not a generic form letter. Each letter cites the actual project in your area, relevant state and local statutes, environmental data, and the specific concerns you selected.

Delivered to their inbox, not spam

Sent from our verified domain with authenticated authenticated records. Commissioners actually see it — unlike personal emails that often get filtered or buried.

Permanent public record

Emails to county commissioners are public records in every state. Your opposition is documented permanently and gets counted when they vote.

Questions.

Is it actually sent to my commissioners?
Yes. We email every county commissioner in your district. You get a copy of the letter and the email addresses it was sent to.
Is it a form letter?
No. Each letter uses your address, your county's project details, and the concerns you selected.
Does this work in my state?
Yes. Any county in any state. Enter your ZIP and we find your commissioners, your state's laws, and the project near you.
What if my county doesn't have an active project?
We'll write a preemptive letter asking your commissioners to adopt data center development guidelines before a proposal arrives. Getting on record early is the strongest position.
Do I need to show up to a hearing?
No. The letter does the work. If you show up too, the written letter is already on the record — it strengthens your position either way.
Why $9?
This site is run by one person. The $9 covers the time to research your county's commissioners, write a personalized letter, and send it. You can send as many as you want — each one is a separate email hitting their inbox.
Why send more than one letter?
Commissioners count emails. One letter puts you on record. Multiple letters over time show sustained opposition — the kind that makes them think twice before voting yes. It's the same reason petition campaigns send repeated messages, not one.

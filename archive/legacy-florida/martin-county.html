Martin County: Silver Fox + the Tesoro Groves PUD

Residents beat one data center in Indiantown and got another door opened anyway. Silver Fox — a 2-million-square-foot hyperscale AI campus on 606 acres, filed by Silver Fox 606 LLC of Juno Beach — was withdrawn by its backers after sustained community opposition. Two weeks later, on April 30, 2026, the Village Council unanimously approved FPL's Tesoro Groves PUD, a 5,722-acre rezoning that explicitly permits data-processing and data-center uses. Council declined resident demands to write a data center ban into it. A lawsuit challenging the rezoning was dismissed with prejudice on July 2, 2026 — it cannot be refiled.

The Full Story

Two land-use actions, one overlap.

Silver Fox (Silver Fox 606 LLC)
Withdrawn by applicant
Village of Indiantown, Martin County 606 acres · ~2M sq ft proposed

The entity behind the Silver Fox proposal is Silver Fox 606 LLC, a Juno Beach-based company founded in April 2025. The principal is Nelson Ferreira of Ferreira Construction. The project targets approximately 606 acres of mostly undeveloped land at 13820 Silver Fox Road inside the Village of Indiantown. The project name comes directly from the street. Unlike Palm Beach County's Project Tango — which relies on land first approved for commercial use in 2016 — the Silver Fox parcel was purchased in June 2025 for $17.5 million, well after the current wave of AI data center development began.

As publicly reported, the plan calls for more than 2 million square feet of data center building across four data center structures up to five stories tall, plus a separate AI lab. The developer has claimed approximately 400 jobs once complete — a figure that is itself the subject of debate in comparable proposals, given that operational hyperscale facilities typically employ relatively few on-site technicians and rely heavily on remote operations. Operator identity, cooling system, power source, and water plan have not been publicly disclosed as of April 2026.

Existing zoning in the area permits heavy industrial development, which is a significant procedural difference from projects that require comprehensive-plan amendments. That mattered while the application was live: Silver Fox needed no comprehensive-plan amendment, so it faced a shorter path than projects like Palm Beach County's Project Tango, which needed one and was ultimately denied 5–1 on July 15, 2026. Silver Fox never reached a public hearing. The applicant withdrew it before one was scheduled, and Silver Fox 606 LLC did not respond to interview requests from local news. The zoning that made the path short is still in place, and the entity still owns the land.

Tesoro Groves PUD (approved April 30, 2026)
Approved 5–0 by Village Council
Indiantown · 5,722 acres P&Z 4/16/2026 · Council approved 4/30/2026

On April 16, 2026, the Village of Indiantown's Planning and Zoning Board approved a rezoning tied to a planned-unit development known as the Tesoro Groves PUD. The rezoning covers approximately 5,722 acres of recently annexed land north and south of Southwest Kanner Highway, west of Southwest Warfield Boulevard. Indiantown Community Development Director Deana Freeman emphasized that the action established a framework, not a final development plan — no buildings, tenants, or infrastructure capacity are reserved at this stage.

What makes the Tesoro Groves action relevant to the Silver Fox fight is that data center uses are explicitly listed among the permitted uses under the PUD framework. FPL representative Jaime Gentile told the board that the framework supports a range of light industrial and utility-related uses — electrical infrastructure, technology facilities, research facilities, data processing centers, and associated FPL utility operations such as substations, transmission, distribution, solar, and battery storage. The framework does not itself authorize a data center; any specific project would still require Village Council review and approval.

Residents at the hearing raised concerns about the types of development that would follow. Resident Linda Biscoe argued that power generation and data centers together would amplify impacts on water, noise, and community resources. Kloee Ciuperger, CEO of the Economic Council of Indiantown, argued the flexibility of the PUD would support the village's long-term economic goals. The Tesoro Groves recommendation still requires Village Council action to become final.

Timeline

How we got here.

April 2025
Silver Fox 606 LLC formed. The Juno Beach-based entity behind the Silver Fox proposal is created, presumably for the purpose of this project. Nelson Ferreira of Ferreira Construction is the principal.
June 2025
Land acquired. Silver Fox 606 LLC purchases approximately 606 acres at 13820 Silver Fox Road in the Village of Indiantown for $17.5 million.
February 2026
Silver Fox proposal surfaces publicly. CBS12 (WPEC) reports that a 2-million-plus-sq-ft AI data center is being proposed in Indiantown. Village Manager Taryn Kryzda says officials want to study similar sites elsewhere to address community concerns. Silver Fox 606 LLC does not respond to interview requests.
March 2026
Silver Fox under staff review. The Village of Indiantown begins staff-level review of the application. Any advancement would require at least three public hearings under Florida land-use procedure.
April 16, 2026
Tesoro Groves PUD recommended. The Village of Indiantown's Planning and Zoning Board approves a rezoning on 5,722 acres of recently annexed land, establishing a PUD framework that explicitly permits data processing centers among allowed uses. The framework is separate from Silver Fox but widens the regulatory door for future data center projects.
April 23, 2026
Council advances the PUD. The Indiantown Village Council takes a step toward adopting the Tesoro Groves zoning ordinance. Residents protest outside before the meeting. FPL says it will cap development when site traffic reaches 7,200 daily vehicle trips — roughly 2 million square feet of built space.
April 2026
Silver Fox withdrawn. Silver Fox 606 LLC pulls its 2-million-plus-sq-ft proposal from consideration after sustained community opposition. The 606 acres at 13820 Silver Fox Road remain zoned for heavy industrial use, and the entity still owns the land.
April 30, 2026
Council approves Tesoro Groves, 5–0. After hours of public comment from a packed chamber, the Village Council unanimously approves FPL's rezoning agreement and master site plan for the 5,722 acres — without language banning future data centers. Resident Daisy Sullivan had urged an explicit prohibition: “The majority do not want data centers here in Indiantown.” Council Member Karen Onsager instead secured a requirement that any non-utility data center pay a significant water deposit toward the village's water plant. FPL's attorney Bob Raynes stressed the vote was zoning only, not a project approval. Data processing centers remain an explicitly permitted use.
May 25, 2026
Residents and a local non-profit sue. A lawsuit challenging the annexation and rezoning of the Tesoro Groves site is filed against both the Village of Indiantown and Florida Power & Light. The filings never use the phrase “data center,” but the contested land is the exact footprint of the debated project.
July 2, 2026
Lawsuit dismissed with prejudice. Attorneys for all parties file a Joint Stipulation of Voluntary Dismissal with Prejudice — the plaintiffs cannot refile the same claims. FPL had argued the plaintiffs lacked standing and that any annexation challenge was time-barred, since the annexation ordinance was adopted February 12 and the suit was not filed until May 25, outside the 30-day window. The rezoning stands.
What It Means

For Indiantown and Martin County residents.

Scale relative to Project Tango

Silver Fox is the largest AI/hyperscale data center proposal in the Treasure Coast region to date. It is larger than Project Tango on both dimensions — 606 acres of land vs. Tango's 202, and 2+ million sq ft of building vs. Tango's reduced 1 million. Silver Fox has not publicly disclosed operator, cooling, or power details that would allow a direct environmental comparison.

The zoning shortcut

One reason Silver Fox is procedurally different from Project Tango: the existing zoning in the area already allows heavy industrial development. Project Tango requires a comprehensive plan amendment to expand its 2016 approval; Silver Fox, in theory, might not. Whether that is correct as a matter of Village code is a question residents and attorneys have standing to test. Village of Indiantown staff review is the current stage.

Why the Tesoro Groves PUD matters

This is exactly what happened. Silver Fox was withdrawn — and the Tesoro Groves PUD passed anyway on April 30, 2026, creating a 5,722-acre framework on adjacent land where data center projects are already listed as permitted uses. Residents asked the Council to write an explicit data center prohibition into the rezoning. It declined. FPL agreed only to cap development at 7,200 daily vehicle trips — roughly 2 million square feet, about the size of the Silver Fox proposal residents just defeated. The procedural hurdle for the next developer is now lower than it was for the last one, and the lawsuit challenging the rezoning was dismissed with prejudice on July 2, 2026.

How to participate

Indiantown is an incorporated village with its own council and planning board, separate from Martin County's Board of County Commissioners. Land-use decisions inside the Village — including both Silver Fox and Tesoro Groves — happen at the Village of Indiantown, not Martin County. Residents who want input should track the Indiantown Village Council agenda and Planning and Zoning Board meeting schedule, and speak during public comment at the relevant hearings.

Developer names, entity filings, and purchase prices for Silver Fox are public records — traceable through Florida Division of Corporations and Martin County Property Appraiser lookups. When a project doesn't respond to press, the paper trail usually answers the questions the developer won't.

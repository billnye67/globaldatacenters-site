Data centers in Angelina County, Texas: 2 projects mapped

2 projects tracked · 2 proposed or being built ·
2,120 MW across all 2 active projects

Angelina County's main data center proposal would reuse the old paper mill on State Highway 103 east of Lufkin, a heavy industrial site that employed nearly 600 people before it closed in 2004. County Judge Keith Wright announced the negotiations in his State of the County address in February 2026, naming the developer as Amp Z and calling it a $1.5 billion project, the largest single investment in the county's history.

Wright said it could bring about $4.6 million in county property taxes at full value, 500 construction jobs and 30 permanent ones, and would need tens of megawatts at first and hundreds over time; the company was negotiating power contracts with the Longleaf generating plant and had agreements with Oncor. A Lufkin outlet, the936.com, reported the company's own phasing: about 175 megawatts by 2026, 1.1 gigawatts by 2028 and 2.1 gigawatts once complete. The site covers more than 1,000 acres outside city limits, so Lufkin gets no tax revenue from it.

Read more

Residents organized as soon as the news broke, objecting to light, noise, air and water pollution, property values and any tax abatement. In May they were barred from speaking about it at a Lufkin City Council meeting because it was not on the agenda. When dozens came to a Commissioners Court meeting in June, Wright told them the county could not stop it: "We have no authority to do a moratorium or stop any type of development in the county. Texas legislators have consciously limited what counties can do, and they've done it on purpose. They don't trust us." His only leverage, he said, is the tax abatement Denver-based AmpZ Champion Data Center Holdings wants, which he would tie to noise limits, landscape buffers and cleanup bonds. Shelley Tatum, a Democratic candidate for Texas House District 9, urged the court to refuse any abatement, predicting the building would stand empty within ten years.

A second project went ahead with no county vote. HyperDataGrid, a Texas company that builds 20-megawatt liquid-cooled facilities, is developing a data center on the former Northern Chip Mill site on Ben Dunn Road off Highway 103 West; Wright said it had secured power and water agreements and begun clearing land.

On July 14, 2026 commissioners voted unanimously for Resolution 010-26, asking the Legislature to let counties set buffer zones, setbacks, noise and light standards, water impact statements and mandatory public hearings for data centers and battery storage. Lufkin passed a supporting resolution in August. The Legislature does not meet in regular session until January 12, 2027. Commissioners have since hired the law firm Allison, Bass & Magee for help with data center abatement agreements; Wright said the county has signed no nondisclosure agreements.

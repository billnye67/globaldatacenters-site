Data centers in Massachusetts: 6 projects mapped

6 projects tracked · 5 operating · 1 stopped or paused ·
33 MW from the 2 active projects that report a figure
· 2 moratoriums and bans in effect

Massachusetts' main data center fight is in Lowell, where Markley Group runs a 350,000-square-foot data center in a residential neighborhood and wants to grow. The City Council voted 10-0 in March for a one-year moratorium on new data center construction, the state's first. On August 10 a Superior Court judge stopped Markley from installing four more diesel backup generators while residents' lawsuit against the state's air permit goes forward; the four already installed can keep running.

Neighbors worry about diesel fumes, noise and water and power use; union electricians who work at the site spoke for the company, which employs about 100 people there. "I'm faced with a dilemma: Which one of my children do I like the best? Do I like the neighbors or do I like the union?" Councilor Rita Mercier said before voting yes.

Read more

The state's largest proposal, a $4 billion campus by Servistar Realties in Westfield, was approved years ago but has stalled; Mayor Mike McCabe said in March "we are still in a holding pattern," and Westfield has since passed a one-year moratorium on further projects. Malden has a one-year moratorium too. Holyoke banned new data centers using more than 12 megawatts of power, Mansfield capped them at 2 megawatts, Southborough at 50,000 square feet, and Everett at 20,000 square feet or 5 megawatts in its Docklands district.

Under an executive order Gov. Maura Healey signed September 8, a data center must reach a community benefits agreement with its host town before state permitting begins. "Unless a community says yes to a data center, we're saying no," Healey said. A West Springfield activist has filed a petition aiming for the 2028 ballot that would require two-thirds of local voters to approve any data center.

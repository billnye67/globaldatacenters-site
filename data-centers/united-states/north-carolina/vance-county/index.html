Data centers in Vance County, North Carolina: 1 project mapped

1 project tracked · 1 proposed or being built ·
900 MW reported
· 1 moratorium or ban in effect

Both are true here. A moratorium on new data centers is in force, and one project is still proposed or being built. A pause usually stops new applications; projects filed before it, or outside its reach, can carry on. Each project page says where it stands.

A rezoning that Vance County approved in April without the word "data center" in it has turned into a 900-megawatt proposal, enough power for several hundred thousand homes. In April 2026 the county commissioners voted 6-1 to rezone a 40-acre parcel on U.S. 158 Business near Henderson, between Pine Meadow Trail and Horseshoe Bend Road, from Employment and Institutional to Light Industrial for Natelli Investments, a Maryland company that had just withdrawn a 250-megawatt data center plan in Apex after opposition, DCD and WRAL reported. Natelli did not confirm the land was for a data center. "You just sold out," one woman shouted at the board; the commission declined to say who had cast the lone vote against.

Residents pressed for a pause. Ahead of a listening session hosted by Gov. Josh Stein's office, they raised energy bills, water use and noise, noting the site's proximity to homes, a school and an animal shelter. "We're not anti-progress in Vance County," one resident told WRAL. "We want our voices to be heard, but we have felt that they have been completely ignored." Petitions against the project and for a one-year moratorium gathered hundreds of signatures.

Read more

In September, as the county discussed a moratorium and new data center rules, Natelli Holdings filed a development plan. In a Sept. 9 letter signed by Tom Natelli, the company said the uncertainty was "very problematic for us at this stage of our efforts. We therefore reluctantly made the decision today to file a plan for development of the site," and attached a marked-up copy of the county's draft ordinance with its preferred changes on setbacks, buffers, cooling and decommissioning, WIZS reported. The Henderson Dispatch described the project as a four-million-square-foot, 900-megawatt campus off U.S. 158 and Poplar Creek Road, in what was once Triangle North Vance. Because Natelli filed first, it has secured "permit choice" under state law, allowing it to choose which rules to follow, the Dispatch reported.

On Sept. 14, 2026 the commissioners adopted a 60-day moratorium on new data center proposals, which many speakers said was too short; it runs through Nov. 13, 2026, when the board can extend it or let new rules take effect. Natelli has said it has been talking with Duke Energy about power and will hold community meetings at an appropriate time. Its end user, water use and schedule have not been disclosed.

Data centers in Fayette County, Georgia: 3 projects mapped

3 projects tracked · 2 proposed or being built · 1 stopped or paused ·
96 MW from the one active project that reports a figure

Fayetteville has one data center campus, and it has decided that is enough. QTS is building what Atlanta News First called Georgia's largest data center on about 600 acres along Highway 54 West between Veterans Parkway and Tyrone Road, 22 miles south of downtown Atlanta: up to 13 buildings and about 6.2 million square feet, with construction expected to run until about 2030. QTS estimates the campus will generate $150 million to $200 million a year in real property tax and about $170 million in sales tax, figures the city repeats on its own website. On March 5, 2026 the City Council adopted Ordinance 26-0-12, which makes new data centers a prohibited use in every zoning district in the city.

The ban's first casualty was a second project. Crow Holdings Development and CHI/Acquisitions wanted a single two-story, 300,000-square-foot data center and a substation on a 37.4-acre parcel on Highway 85 North, just past the Fayette Pavilion shopping center. The land was already zoned for it, so the developer's only obstacle was the Planning and Zoning Commission, which rejected the site plan on January 27. The developer appealed to the council, then withdrew the appeal on March 18, the day before the hearing. "I believe the people have spoken and CHI listened," Councilman Emmett Spurlock told The Citizen, crediting a town hall the council had held. No capacity was ever disclosed, and what will now be built on the site is not known.

Read more

The fight that shaped both decisions was over QTS's water. In May 2025, after a neighbor near the Annelise Park subdivision complained of low water pressure, the county water director, Vanessa Tigert, wrote to QTS that a meter had been installed "without the knowledge or inspection" of county staff, and the county billed the company about $147,000. When the letter surfaced through a records request in 2026, the county walked it back: "Our letter was unclear." County Administrator Steve Rapson told The Citizen that staff had inspected the meter but believed it was being read electronically during a switch to smart meters, and that it was not. "It's not like they put a meter in, threw a camel net over it, and we didn't know," he said. QTS paid, and says it has stayed within its permitted estimates. Atlanta News First's analysis of billing records found the county had billed QTS for 109 million gallons across 13 water lines since February 2024, which QTS says went on concrete, dust control and site preparation.

The county and QTS say the construction demand will fall away. QTS says each finished building will use about as much water as four American households, because its closed-loop cooling recirculates the same water, and the county says QTS uses less than 1 percent of its daily production. Neighbors are unconvinced. "If my daughter down here tapped into that big pipeline down the road without putting a meter on it, she'd be in jail," Willard Neal, 87, who lives near the site, told Atlanta News First. "A data center is not going to jail." He called living beside it "terrible," citing tree clearing, construction and a constant hum. A few miles away in Tyrone, the county's other approved project, Microsoft's Project Rita Campus, is moving ahead at 96 megawatts of power, the only other capacity figure reported in the county.

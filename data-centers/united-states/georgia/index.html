Data centers in Georgia: 105 projects mapped

105 projects tracked · 60 proposed or being built · 28 operating · 13 stopped or paused · 4 with no stage on record ·
13,563 MW from the 40 active projects that report a figure
· 29 moratoriums and bans in effect

Georgia's biggest data center plan is Project Camellia, a campus of about 1,400 acres near Rincon in Effingham County that Octans Georgia, an OpenAI affiliate, plans to build. It could need up to 3.2 gigawatts (3,200 megawatts) of power. The county commission was due to vote on the plan September 15, then gave itself more time; no new date has been announced, and two lawsuits over the approvals are pending in Effingham County Superior Court.

In September, five residents and the environmental group One Hundred Miles sued the county's Industrial Development Authority, saying its July vote to commit the land to Octans and give it a 50% property tax break went ahead without proper notice or a meaningful agenda. Three other residents sued September 2 over the August zoning change that allows data centers on industrial land, saying the required hearings were skipped. "We bought into that neighborhood, into that area, because we wanted the quiet right to enjoyment," plaintiff Raymond Carver, who lives near the site, told the Effingham Herald.

Read more

Power is the statewide question. In December 2025 state regulators voted 5-0 to let Georgia Power add about 10,000 megawatts, 80% of it for data centers, at a construction cost of $16.3 billion; commission staff said customers would pay $50 billion to $60 billion over the coming decades. Georgia Power's chief executive, Chris Womack, told shareholders that once the OpenAI contract is confirmed, the utility will have promised about one gigawatt more than it has permission to provide.

Most other projects are around Atlanta and in the counties west and south of it. In Augusta, residents of the Haynes Station subdivision are fighting QTS's data center on Gordon Highway, an expanded version of a project approved before their homes were built; the commission's 49-day pause in June did not cover it. Local governments from Cobb and DeKalb counties to Valdosta and Albany now have 31 pauses on new data centers in force.

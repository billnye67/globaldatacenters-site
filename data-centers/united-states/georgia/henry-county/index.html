Data centers in Henry County, Georgia: 3 projects mapped

3 projects tracked · 3 proposed or being built ·
240 MW from the one active project that reports a figure

Henry County's first approved data center is in the city of Hampton, about 30 miles south of Atlanta. Hampton granted Southeast Property Holdings LLC a conditional use permit in 2025 for three buildings of 195,000 square feet each and a substation, on 133 acres at Georgia 20 and McDonough Street, part of the long-unbuilt Henderson Farms development. The developer is responsible for negotiating its own power supply. The city's conditions require a 100-foot buffer, landscaping to screen the buildings, a noise study, green roofs, water-saving measures, $75,000 to the city and land along the Towaliga River for a park.

Two larger rezoning requests for unincorporated Henry County, both tied to the developer Red Wolf DCD Properties, split the county's zoning advisory board in November 2024: one was recommended for 148 acres at Georgia 20 and Rocky Creek Road, for a 1.2 million-square-foot campus, and one was recommended for denial on 249.8 acres off Simpson Mill Road, for 1.25 million square feet in two buildings. Commissioners pulled both requests from their November 19, 2024 agenda and pushed them to 2025, and neither has come back for a vote since.

Read more

More is planned around Hampton. Equinix is developing a 240-megawatt, four-building campus in the area, and Hampton Technology Park Owner LLC has filed a regional-impact application for a 603-acre campus on Lower Woolsey Road of up to five buildings and 4 million square feet, to be built out through 2033. In August 2026 construction near South Lee Road and Lower Woolsey Road drew protest because it sits next to a historic Black cemetery with 54 documented names and 16 undocumented enslaved people. "It hurts. It hurts. We're out here advocating and trying to save these cemeteries and they're constantly ignoring us," said Shawndrea Gay of the preservation effort. The city set a 50-foot buffer, and Mayor Ann Tarpley said the goal is to get the cemetery on the state and national historic registers.

The county is trying to catch up. The Henry County Water Authority now requires any business using more than 100,000 gallons a day to pay for its own feasibility study. A draft county ordinance would allow data centers only in the M-1 and M-2 manufacturing districts, with a 200-foot landscape buffer, a 500-foot setback from non-industrial land, a 1,000-foot setback from homes, noise capped at 55 decibels by day and 45 at night, no open evaporative cooling, and 30 percent open space on each site. "I'm all about something that is going to support us economically, but at the same time, not off the backs of our constituents for their quality of life," Commissioner Neat Robinson said. Both the county's revised ordinance, due back for a vote in February 2026, and Hampton's proposed 120-day data center moratorium, set for a vote the same month, were scheduled for then; no result has been published for either.

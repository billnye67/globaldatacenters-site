Data centers in Wright County, Minnesota: 1 project mapped

1 project tracked · 1 proposed or being built ·
No power figure reported for active projects
· 1 moratorium or ban in effect

Both are true here. A moratorium on new data centers is in force, and one project is still proposed or being built. A pause usually stops new applications; projects filed before it, or outside its reach, can carry on. Each project page says where it stands.

The main data center fight in Wright County is in Monticello, where Monticello Tech LLC, a group tied to developer Nick Frattalone, wants to build a campus of up to 3 million square feet on about 550 acres south of 85th Street and east of Highway 25. The site is in Monticello Township but inside an orderly annexation area that allows it to be annexed into the city. A second developer, Scannell Properties, has floated four buildings on about 106 acres near Otter Creek and the Bertram Chain of Lakes Regional Park.

On April 27, 2026, the Monticello City Council voted 4-1 to adopt a Data Center Planned Unit Development ordinance, with council member Charlotte Gabler voting no. The ordinance creates a zoning district for data centers. Developers must get a rezoning and then a conditional use permit, show there is enough water and energy, and meet standards for buffers, noise and lighting. The council raised the minimum setback from homes to 300 feet, which opponents said was not enough. MPR News reported that the ordinance also rules out city tax incentives and makes developers pay for any new infrastructure. About a dozen people spoke at the meeting, nearly all opposed. Jenna Van Den Boom, an organizer of Stop the Monticello Data Centers, called the vote "a kick in the gut." Mayor Lloyd Hilgart said, "I believe that everything but the kitchen sink is in that ordinance."

Read more

Monticello Tech filed its formal applications for a conditional use permit, rezoning and preliminary plat on July 6, 2026. The city found them incomplete, and a revised submission in August was ruled complete. The Monticello Times reported that the filing describes up to eight buildings on about 547 acres, built in phases over four to eight years. It estimates about 75,000 gallons of water a day using dry cooling and about $45.9 million in public infrastructure that the applicant says the developer or the eventual operator would pay for. No end user has been named. Electricity use is not quantified. Xcel Energy would serve the site through a new substation on its 345-kilovolt system, with transmission upgrades at the developer's expense. The Planning Commission, which is advisory only, has a public hearing set for October 6, 2026, and the city says it must decide by December 8, 2026.

The Minnesota Center for Environmental Advocacy sued Monticello in Wright County District Court in February 2026, arguing that the city's Alternative Urban Areawide Review of the site did not adequately evaluate environmental impacts. Under an agreement between the parties, Monticello Tech can keep pursuing permits but must give 90 days' written notice before any grading or site work. A pretrial conference is set for December 29.

Outside the cities, the Wright County Board of Commissioners unanimously approved an emergency moratorium on May 19, 2026. County Ordinance 26-2 pauses applications to build data centers in all of the county's 18 townships, including townships that do their own planning and zoning, and gives the county up to one year to study the issue and adopt rules on the time, place and manner in which data centers would be allowed. Fourteen people spoke at the public hearing, all in favor of the moratorium. Commissioner Kirby Moynagh said the county needed to be "proactive instead of reactive." Board Chair Darek Vetsch cautioned that the pause is not a ban: "If you create an ordinance that says you shall not build a data center in Wright County, we will get litigated and we will lose." The moratorium does not change Monticello's ordinance.

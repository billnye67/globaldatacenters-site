Data centers in Minnesota: 17 projects mapped

17 projects tracked · 7 proposed or being built · 2 operating · 7 stopped or paused · 1 with no stage on record ·
1,075 MW from the 5 active projects that report a figure
· 11 moratoriums and bans in effect

Minnesota's largest data center plans are Google's campuses in Pine Island, about 20 minutes northwest of Rochester, and Hermantown, outside Duluth, plus Tract's 2.5 million-square-foot campus in Farmington. All three face lawsuits. Pine Island's project is on hold after a Goodhue County judge ordered developer Ryan Companies on May 22 to stop all construction and pre-construction work until the case is decided. Hermantown's still needs city council approval, and the city is redoing its environmental review, expected in September or October.

The Minnesota Center for Environmental Advocacy argues that the cities' environmental reviews did not properly study projects they knew were likely to be very large data centers. Google has promised Pine Island's schools $24 million over 20 years and Hermantown's about $70 million over 28, and the two cities offer tax breaks worth more than $100 million combined. "I think it's a bribe, honestly," said Pine Island resident Megan Post. In Hermantown, leaked recordings of city officials mocking opponents led the mayor to apologize and seek an outside investigation.

Read more

In Farmington, residents are suing over Tract's campus, which would need about 700 megawatts of power and more than double the city's water use of about 2.14 million gallons a day; a judge refused to dismiss the case in November 2025. In Monticello, the same advocacy group is challenging the review for a planned data center, and the city now requires it to meet a new data center ordinance before permits.

At least eight cities and counties have paused new data centers, including Eagan, Rosemount, Inver Grove Heights and Wright County. Minneapolis voted in May to pause data centers larger than 350,000 square feet, then approved a five-month moratorium June 25. "My constituents and people from across this city are so, so clear that they don't want data centers at all," said Council Member Soren Stevenson. Local boards have rejected data centers in Apple Valley, Elk River and Nobles County, and a developer dropped its plan for the former Star Tribune printing plant in Minneapolis.

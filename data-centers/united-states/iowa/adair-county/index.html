Data centers in Adair County, Iowa: 1 project mapped

1 project tracked · 1 proposed or being built ·
200 MW reported
· 1 moratorium or ban in effect

Both are true here. A moratorium on new data centers is in force, and one project is still proposed or being built. A pause usually stops new applications; projects filed before it, or outside its reach, can carry on. Each project page says where it stands.

Applied Digital, which already operates a 180-megawatt site in Ellendale, North Dakota, proposed a 200-megawatt data center in Adair County in 2024: two 300,000-square-foot buildings beside a NextEra Energy substation south of Adair, with 40 to 70 employees. After residents objected, the county began drafting a zoning ordinance in April 2024 to set the building back from the road and neighboring properties.

The county adopted a moratorium on data centers, high-density computing and battery storage on March 18, 2026, when the Board of Supervisors voted 5-0 for Resolution 2026-26, effective immediately with no set end date. A week later, Supervisor Jodie Hoadley presented an updated draft ordinance, prompted by a planned high-voltage line through the Hebron area, that would set environmental and noise restrictions rather than bar developers outright. She said she had shared the draft with Union and Madison counties, and that Union County planned its own moratorium.

There is no public word yet on whether Applied Digital still wants the Fallow Avenue site, or whether the county has adopted a permanent ordinance since the moratorium took effect.

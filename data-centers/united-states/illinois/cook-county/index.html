Data centers by place

Data centers in Cook County, Illinois: 25 projects mapped

25 projects tracked · 9 proposed or being built · 12 operating · 2 stopped or paused · 2 with no stage on record ·
1,107 MW from the 12 active projects that report a figure

Chicago's mayor wants to stop new data centers in the city, and the fight over whether he can is now the county's main data center story. On August 11, 2026, Mayor Brandon Johnson called for a temporary moratorium on new or expanded data centers until permanent rules are written, and signed an executive order requiring closer review of diesel generator air permits, noise limits on generators and cooling equipment, and an assessment of power demand, water use and neighborhood effects for planned developments. "Chicagoans have been absolutely clear," he said. A moratorium needs a City Council vote, and Zoning Committee Chair Gilbert Villegas (36th) called the order "another political stunt by the mayor." Chicago has 39 data centers, most in or near downtown, and Cook County has 109 with 12 more planned, part of what CBRE ranks as the country's fourth-largest data center market.

The sharpest local anger is on the Southeast Side. Related Digital announced in March 2025 that it would build a hyperscale data center of about 1 gigawatt at the north end of Quantum Shore, the $9 billion quantum computing development on the former U.S. Steel South Works site, which is getting $500 million in state money and an estimated $175 million in county tax breaks over 30 years. At a Southside Together town hall at Arnold Mireles Academy in August, the crowd chanted "Shut it down" and booed Johnson when he declined to halt the project. "This facility threatens our peace," said South Chicago resident Reneé Redmond, 67. Johnson said no data center is planned as part of the campus, and the city's business affairs department said it had no pending license application for the data center site. Lia Terrell of Southside Together, who has lived in the area for 15 years, said the group would keep organizing "with or without the mayor."

The county's biggest concentration of data centers is not downtown but in the suburb of Elk Grove Village, near O'Hare, where ten projects on this record share an industrial corridor, including campuses run by Aligned, Digital Realty, Microsoft, Prime, STACK, Stream and T5. Most have not said publicly what stage they are at or how much power they will use.

In the suburbs the fights are site by site. In Hoffman Estates, Karis Critical wanted to rezone 186 acres at 4800 West Higgins Road, and on June 30, 2026 the landowner withdrew the application after residents learned that village staff had discussed it with the developer for about 18 months before it was made public, and that Village Manager Eric Palm had signed a letter pledging staff support. In July, Cicero's trustees passed resolutions opposing two rumored data centers, including one at Hawthorne Race Course in neighboring Stickney, saying they want none built anywhere near the town of more than 80,000 people.

The costs are what residents keep raising. ComEd customers' monthly charges rose about 12 percent in June, after double-digit increases the summer before, and ComEd's chief executive said in June that proposed data centers in Northern Illinois had requested 33 gigawatts. "If we're just looking at the impacts that data centers have on our electricity costs, there's not much the city of Chicago can do," said Sarah Moskowitz, executive director of the Citizen's Utility Board, who wants the state to act. Springfield has not: the POWER Act, which would have made data centers report water use, sign community benefit agreements and pay for their own renewable power, died without a vote when the session ended on May 31, and Gov. JB Pritzker paused new state data center tax incentives from July 1. State Sen. Darby Hills of Barrington Hills has filed Senate Bill 1050, which would bar data centers within three miles of a residential community without its consent.

The City Council still has to decide whether to take up Johnson's moratorium. Southside Together held a rally at 91st Street and Commercial Avenue on September 12, and both the POWER Act and Hills's bill are expected back in the fall veto session; until then, nothing proposed in Cook County is subject to the water reporting, community agreements or energy requirements the POWER Act contained.

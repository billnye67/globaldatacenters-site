Data centers in California: 55 projects mapped

55 projects tracked · 18 proposed or being built · 23 operating · 9 stopped or paused · 5 with no stage on record ·
1,551 MW from the 25 active projects that report a figure
· 4 moratoriums and bans in effect

California has 16 data center projects proposed or under construction, the most of them in Santa Clara County, but the sharpest local fights are in the Imperial and Coachella valleys. On September 9 an Imperial County judge overturned the county's approval of developer Sebastian Rucci's nearly 1-million-square-foot data center complex next to the Victoria Ranch neighborhood near the city of Imperial, and ordered a full environmental review. Rucci said he would seek a new trial and, failing that, appeal.

Imperial County supervisors paused all data center projects on county land in June and later extended the pause through May 2027; the city of Imperial did the same. A county advisory committee is due to report on zoning rules in January. "We are disappointed but not deterred," Rucci told KPBS after the ruling.

Read more

In the Coachella Valley, Coachella became the first Riverside County city to ban data centers permanently, after residents fought Stronghold Power Systems' planned campus at Avenue 52 and Fillmore Street. Indio extended its moratorium through October 16 while it drafts a ban, Desert Hot Springs extended its pause to two years, and Palm Springs adopted a 45-day moratorium on August 26.

In Los Angeles County, Monterey Park voters approved a permanent ban on June 2, making it the first US city to ban data centers outright; the developer had already withdrawn. In Santa Clara, finished data centers built by Digital Realty and Stack have sat empty waiting for Silicon Valley Power to finish a $450 million grid upgrade due in 2028.

On September 21 Gov. Gavin Newsom signed seven bills that make data centers pay for the grid upgrades they cause and disclose expected water use to local governments. Indio's council was scheduled to take up its permanent ban in September; no result has been published, and its moratorium runs through October 16.

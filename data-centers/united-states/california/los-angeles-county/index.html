Both are true here. Monterey Park has a ban on new data centers in force, and one project is still proposed or being built. A pause usually stops new applications; projects filed before it, or outside its reach, can carry on. Each project page says where it stands.

Los Angeles County has banned new large AI data centers in its unincorporated areas. On September 17, 2026, Regional Planning Director Amy Bodek ordered an immediate prohibition as interim guidance while the county writes it into the zoning code, following a motion by Board Chair Hilda Solis that supervisors approved unanimously in April. Solis says she will bring the zoning changes to the Board of Supervisors; no date has been set.

The push started in Monterey Park, where HMC StratCap proposed a data center of about 250,000 square feet at the vacant Saturn Business Park, 1977 Saturn Street. After packed meetings, the city adopted a 45-day moratorium in January, the developer withdrew in April, and on June 2 voters passed Measure NDC, which bans data centers unless future voters repeal it. "We had a victory landslide," said Steven Kung, co-founder of No Data Center Monterey Park. El Monte and Baldwin Park voted in April for yearlong bans.

Read more

Residents in Rowland Heights, Hacienda Heights, La Puente and other nearby communities are now fighting a possible data center at the mostly vacant Puente Hills Mall in the City of Industry, which has about 250 residents. In El Segundo, developer Eight Form withdrew a 50-megawatt plan to replace a Hyatt Place hotel at 750 N. Nash Street on July 9, after three hours of opposition at the planning commission.

Most of the county's data centers sit in El Segundo, downtown Los Angeles and Vernon, which runs its own power utility. In Vernon, Goodman Group has begun building LAX01, a 49.5-megawatt, three-level data center on 5.6 acres, and Digital Realty has bought land for a 32-megawatt building.

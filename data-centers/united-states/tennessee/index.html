Data centers in Tennessee: 14 projects mapped

14 projects tracked · 5 proposed or being built · 7 operating · 1 stopped or paused · 1 with no stage on record ·
1,829 MW from the 8 active projects that report a figure
· 23 moratoriums and bans in effect

Tennessee's biggest data centers are Elon Musk's Colossus and Colossus II in Memphis, now owned by SpaceX, which has bought more than 1,100 acres across the Memphis area and is building a gas power plant across the state line in Southaven, Mississippi. Across the rest of the state, towns and counties are pausing new data centers: more than 20 have moratoriums in force, and Nashville's runs until December 1.

Colossus II sits beside homes in Whitehaven. "You just have to endure this," said Loretta Thornton, who lives next door. "Who will want to buy it, living beside this thing?" Memphis Mayor Paul Young says the company paid $25 million in taxes in its first year, second only to FedEx in Shelby County, but acknowledged it has not delivered everything it promised, including a delayed water recycling plant meant to cut its use of city water for cooling. Southaven residents have filed a class action over noise from the power plant.

Read more

Nashville's Metro Council passed its moratorium on July 21 along with the city's first data center zoning rules, after DC Blox's plan next to the Nashville Zoo drew an uproar; Mayor Freddie O'Connell proposed buying that site by eminent domain because the project applied before the rules passed. In East Tennessee, Knoxville paused permits for data centers of 10 megawatts or more for a year on July 7, Knox County passed a one-year moratorium in June, and Anderson and Morgan counties chose two years. McMinnville's 18-month pause followed a proposed 25-megawatt data center near homes.

The Tennessee Valley Authority's new, higher power rate for data centers takes effect October 1 and will raise their bills by about 10% on average over three years.

Data centers in Shelby County, Tennessee: 3 projects mapped

3 projects tracked · 1 proposed or being built · 2 operating ·
1,286 MW from the 2 active projects that report a figure

The Memphis fight is not about whether a data center should be built. It is about whether the gas turbines powering it needed a permit, and it has been going on for two years.

The Greater Memphis Chamber announced in June 2024 that xAI would build a supercomputer in Memphis. Shortly after, the company began installing methane gas turbines at the site that became Colossus 1, without a permit. xAI applied for a permit for 15 turbines in January 2025, but aerial photos that April found 35 running. After a 60-day notice of intent to sue filed on behalf of the NAACP, xAI removed the unpermitted turbines in June 2025; the health department approved a 15-turbine permit that July anyway. The EPA closed the loophole in January 2026, ruling that turbines need permits even when called temporary or portable. The NAACP, represented by SELC and Earthjustice, sued xAI under the Clean Air Act in April 2026 and sought an injunction to shut the turbines down in May.

Read more

Colossus 1 runs at 340 megawatts and Colossus 2, which is expanding, at 946 megawatts; Colossus 2 sits at Tulane Road in Whitehaven, a historic Black neighborhood in southwest Memphis. In September 2026 the council discussed spending nearly $3.3 million in tax revenue from the xAI site on home repairs, blight cleanup and air-quality monitoring within five miles of the property.

The council is also considering a one-year pause on new data center rezoning, site plans and building permits, sponsored by council chair Jana Swearengen-Washington, Yolanda Cooper-Sutton and former chair J.B. Smiley. A committee advanced it August 18, 2026. The full council is scheduled to vote on it October 6, 2026.

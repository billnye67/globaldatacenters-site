Data centers in New York: 35 projects mapped

35 projects tracked · 11 proposed or being built · 18 operating · 4 stopped or paused · 2 with no stage on record ·
2,609 MW from the 20 active projects that report a figure
· 17 moratoriums and bans in effect

New York has stopped issuing state environmental permits for new data centers that can use 50 megawatts of power or more. Gov. Kathy Hochul's Executive Order 62, signed July 14, holds them for about a year while the state studies what these projects do to the grid, water and air. Smaller projects, and those that need only local approvals, still go through town boards.

The biggest test is in Massena, in St. Lawrence County, where North Country Collocation Services wants to build a 1.5 million-square-foot campus near the St. Lawrence River that would use about 400 megawatts from the New York Power Authority. The company says the state pause does not cover it. On September 16 the town board voted 3-2 against a six-month local moratorium, with Supervisor Raymond Lancto and Kristy Baker in the minority. "We need a moratorium," said Marina Johnson Zafiris of Akwesasne, whose group, Mohawks United in Safety and Health, leads the opposition.

Read more

Niagara County has the most tracked sites, most of them already running, including TeraWulf's Lake Mariner campus in Barker and data centers in North Tonawanda and Niagara Falls, where Niagara Falls Redevelopment has proposed a Niagara Digital Campus. Manhattan's are older buildings such as 60 Hudson Street and 111 Eighth Avenue. Near Syracuse, more than 350 people turned out against Ranalli's proposed 300-megawatt data center in Lysander, and the town passed a six-month moratorium on May 7. In the Town of Tonawanda, hundreds opposed a 300-megawatt campus on the former Tonawanda Coke site.

In Rockland County, Orangetown passed a moratorium in August but exempted DataBank's planned expansion in Orangeburg to 45 megawatts, just under the state's line. A bill from State Sen. Kristen Gonzalez that would extend the pause to data centers as small as 20 megawatts passed in June; Hochul has not committed to signing it. Lysander's moratorium runs out November 7.

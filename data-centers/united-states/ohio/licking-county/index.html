Data centers in Licking County, Ohio: 12 projects mapped

12 projects tracked · 4 proposed or being built · 4 operating · 1 stopped or paused · 3 with no stage on record ·
1,420 MW from the 4 active projects that report a figure
· 1 moratorium or ban in effect

Both are true here. St. Albans Township has a moratorium on new data centers in force, and 4 projects are still proposed or being built. A pause usually stops new applications; projects filed before it, or outside its reach, can carry on. Each project page says where it stands.

In the Columbus area, one of the country's top data center markets, a Licking County town has turned one down. On August 25, 2026 Pataskala City Council unanimously rejected a site plan from Aligned Data Centers for a 200-megawatt, three-building campus on about 89 acres at Broad and Mink streets in the city's industrial park, following the planning and zoning commission's 5-2 recommendation on June 3. That hearing ran four hours before a standing-room crowd. Aligned said the land was already zoned for the use, that it sat beside American Electric Power facilities, and that it would not seek or accept local property tax abatements. Maritza Motino, who lives across Broad Street, sat through it holding a "No Data Center" sign listing noise, environmental impact and the cost of electricity. Council President Brandon Galik, who moved to accept the commission's recommendation, said the "plan is incomplete" and he was not comfortable voting for it.

Licking County already hosts one of the country's biggest data center clusters, mostly in New Albany: Meta's 562-megawatt Prometheus campus, Google's 453-megawatt site, and Amazon Web Services' 213-megawatt campus are all operating, with Vantage building a fourth. Microsoft has two projects under construction in the county, in Heath and at New Albany's Beech Road site, alongside more capacity Meta arranged in November 2025 to draw from AEP's Green Chapel substation, built for an Intel chip plant that Intel has since delayed.

The state has pulled back its own incentives at the same time. On June 1, 2026 the Ohio Tax Credit Authority approved a $42.3 million sales tax exemption for Cologix, part of it for a 150-acre site in Johnstown drawing 75 megawatts at first and up to 176, which also has a 15-year, 100 percent property tax abatement from the city. It was the last such exemption for the indefinite future: days earlier Governor Mike DeWine had announced a pause on them, exempting the Cologix project, after reporting that the 2025 breaks had come to $1.568 billion, eleven times what the state had estimated.

What comes next in Pataskala is the ballot: council has agreed to put a proposed data center ban before voters on November 3, 2026, after residents gathered more than 500 signatures.

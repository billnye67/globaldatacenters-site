Data centers in Androscoggin County, Maine: 1 project mapped

1 project tracked · 1 stopped or paused

Lewiston's data center plan lasted six days in public. The owner of the historic Bates Mill No. 3 downtown proposed a $300 million artificial intelligence data center using 85,000 square feet of the building, with a development team, MillCompute LLC, Maine Public and the Bangor Daily News reported. The city estimated it would employ about 30 people; the Bates Mill once housed a TD Bank call center with more than 1,000. The developer asked for a 20-year tax increment financing and credit enhancement agreement that would have returned 90% of the property's taxes for the first 10 years and 85% for years 11 to 20.

The process ran mostly out of sight. City Administrator Bryan Kaenrath signed a non-binding letter of intent on June 5, 2025, after the developer asked for confidentiality for competitive reasons; the developer submitted its proposal in September, council members said they did not see the details until November, and after two closed-door sessions the public got less than a week's notice before the Dec. 16 vote, the Bangor Daily News reported. The vote was set for then partly because the developer needed a tenant quickly for the long-empty building, which was burdening him with taxes, maintenance and loan costs; lenders now own it.

Read more

Residents filled the council chamber to standing room only. "I'm 45 and I remember driving over the bridge and holding my breath because it smelled so bad," said Sarah Demarest, who helped lead the opposition, of the Androscoggin River, since cleaned up. "It felt like the data center plan was a contradiction to everything we had worked for." Councilor Scott Harriman received more than 100 emails and calls; one was in favor. The council voted unanimously against the project, and no one from MillCompute spoke at the meeting. "This had the potential for being a great project," Council President David Chittim said. "It may still have, but not within the time frames and the constraints that have been imposed on it."

"I think the underlying lesson here is that developers need to stop planning these data centers in secret and then complaining about how much they've invested in the process once the public finally becomes aware of it and pushes back," Harriman told the Bangor Daily News. Kaenrath, who had backed the project, said the city would be "quite cautious about future proposals," and developer Bill Johnson did not respond to the Bangor Daily News. The acting economic development director, John Blais, said a few other data center developers had since made inquiries but were put off by the city's longer review process. The statewide moratorium bill those developers feared, LD 307, was vetoed by Governor Janet Mills on April 24, 2026, and lawmakers fell five votes short of overriding her five days later. Lewiston has since adopted its own temporary moratorium on data centers, and Mills' advisory council on data center policy must report to the Legislature by January 29, 2027.

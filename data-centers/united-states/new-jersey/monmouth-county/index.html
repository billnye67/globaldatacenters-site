Data centers in Monmouth County, New Jersey: 1 project mapped

1 project tracked · 1 operating ·
No power figure reported for active projects
· 4 moratoriums and bans in effect

Monmouth County's live data center fight is at Bell Works in Holmdel, the former Bell Labs campus that now houses offices, shops and restaurants. On July 21 the Township Committee introduced a ban on data centers; three days later the property's owner, Somerset Development, filed to build a two-story, 40-megawatt data center on the site, replacing an existing building. The committee adopted the ban unanimously on August 11, but the township's attorney says an applicant is generally entitled to the zoning in effect when its application was filed, and a 2013 redevelopment agreement already lists data centers as a permitted use there.

Founder and chief executive Ralph Zucker says the project is small by today's standards, "less than one-tenth the size of the 300-500-plus megawatt hyperscale data centers driving much of the recent public conversation," and "sits in an isolated portion of the property, out of view from surrounding neighborhoods and roadways." Residents raised power use, noise, water and property values at the August 11 meeting. "We were there to cheer the banning of a data center and it was announced, 'Guess what, guys? The data center is coming,'" said Kin Gee of Better Holmdel. Mayor Rocco Impreveduto said the committee cannot comment on an application pending before the Planning Board.

Read more

Neighboring towns have banned data centers before any were proposed there. Red Bank and Asbury Park passed bans in June, Neptune Township banned both data centers and immigration detention facilities in July, and Howell's council passed a ban in August after an earlier moratorium. None of those towns had a known project. Middletown's committee introduced its own ban on September 8, with a final vote scheduled for September 21; the vote was scheduled for that date and no result has been published.

What comes next: the Holmdel Planning Board still has to hold hearings on the Bell Works site plan, where the question is whether the pre-ban zoning and the 2013 agreement let the 40-megawatt data center go ahead.

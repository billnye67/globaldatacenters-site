Data centers in New Jersey: 21 projects mapped

21 projects tracked · 5 proposed or being built · 12 operating · 4 stopped or paused ·
668 MW from the 5 active projects that report a figure
· 24 moratoriums and bans in effect

New Jersey's biggest data center going up now is DataOne's campus on South Lincoln Avenue in Vineland, a 14-hall "AI factory" of up to 350 megawatts that Nebius will run for Microsoft under a $17.4 billion deal. The company expects construction to finish in late December. On September 22 the Department of Environmental Protection fined DataOne $1.07 million for installing 62 gas generators without air permits and gave it 45 days to get the permits or stop running them.

Neighbors have sued DataOne over noise since ground was broken in the spring, and in August the city halted work after crews began building a liquefied natural gas tank and fuel cells before permits were approved. "This enforcement action... sends a clear message that these facilities will not be constructed or operated with impunity in this state," said Ed Potosnak, the environmental commissioner. In Kenilworth, CoreWeave is building a data center on the former Merck campus off Galloping Hill Road after buying a building and about 27 acres for $322 million.

Read more

Towns have been banning data centers one after another. Pemberton Township was first, in February; by August 20 more than 30 towns had bans on the books, including Phillipsburg, Waterford, Logan, Millville, Bayonne, Jersey City and Howell, while Sayreville chose an 18-month moratorium. Residents stopped projects in Monroe Township, where the council removed data centers from Hexa Builders' plan on the Black Horse Pike, in Andover, in East Greenwich, where American Tower withdrew a 4-megawatt plan after a 5,800-signature petition, and in New Brunswick. Developers have sued Monroe, Andover and Montgomery over their bans.

Gov. Mikie Sherrill opposes a statewide moratorium. On July 7 she signed the Data Center Fair Share Act, which puts large data centers in their own rate class, makes them pay for their own power and grid upgrades, and requires them to cut back before households when the grid is strained. DataOne's 45-day deadline to get its generator permits runs out in early November.

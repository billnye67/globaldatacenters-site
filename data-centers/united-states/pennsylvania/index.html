Data centers in Pennsylvania: 86 projects mapped

18,177 MW from the 31 active projects that report a figure

Amazon is the biggest builder in Pennsylvania, with a pledge to spend $20 billion. It bought an existing data center and about 1,200 acres beside Talen Energy's nuclear plant in Salem Township, Luzerne County, where it plans up to 15 buildings, and plans a second campus in Falls Township, north of Philadelphia. It has applied for a local permit for 36 data centers at Homer City in Indiana County, next to a 4.5-gigawatt gas plant on the site of the state's largest former coal plant.

Local fights have stopped several projects. A 1,300-acre site in Hazle Township that Amazon is involved in was denied by local officials last year, and the developer has appealed. In Columbia Borough, Lancaster County, the council unanimously turned down a $6.4 million offer from Saadia Holdings to build on the former McGinness Airport land after more than 500 residents came to the meeting, organizer Taylor Enterline said. Projects in Blakely have also been blocked or delayed after public opposition.

Read more

Townships across the state have given themselves about six months to write data center rules using a state-law tool called a curative amendment, which works as a pause: Center, Adams and Lancaster townships in Butler County, Brookville in Jefferson County and Granville in Mifflin County among them. Once a township starts the process, it does not have to consider landowners' requests to add data centers to its zoning. In Granville, plans for a data center were filed two days before the vote.

In Harrisburg, the House and Senate each passed a repeal of the state's sales tax break on data center equipment in June, but neither version reached Gov. Josh Shapiro before the budget. The break is projected to cost $188.4 million in 2026-27. The Senate returned September 28 with 11 session days scheduled for the rest of the year.

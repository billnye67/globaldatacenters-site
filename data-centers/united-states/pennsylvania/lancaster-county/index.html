Data centers in Lancaster County, Pennsylvania: 2 projects mapped

2 projects tracked · 2 proposed or being built ·
100 MW from the one active project that reports a figure

Lancaster County's data center story is in the City of Lancaster, where two former R.R. Donnelley/LSC Communications printing plants are becoming what may be the largest investment in the county's history. Machine Investment Group paid $130 million in early 2025 for the plants at 216 Greenfield Road near Route 30 and 1375 Harrisburg Pike next to Long's Park, 144 acres in all. In July 2025, at Sen. Dave McCormick's Pennsylvania Energy and Innovation Summit, CoreWeave announced up to $6 billion for an AI data center there, starting at 100 megawatts with room for 300, co-developed by Chirisa Technology Parks and Machine Investment Group with CoreWeave as sole tenant. The developers put the two campuses' combined construction cost at $10 billion. CoreWeave projects about 600 construction jobs and 70 permanent roles at launch, rising to 175.

The city had little say over whether it happens. Both sites are zoned Suburban Manufacturing, and the city classified data centers as "wholesale trade and storage," a permitted use. "As long as something complies with the law, even if everyone in town hates it, the municipality will have to approve it," said PennFuture attorney Brigitte Meyer. The first phase at Greenfield Road, a reuse of the existing building, needed no land development approval and is under construction; a resident's appeal of the zoning determination was pending in late 2025. In August 2025 the City Council asked its solicitor to draft a data center zoning ordinance that would require zoning hearings and public input.

Read more

Instead of a fight, the city negotiated. On November 20, 2025 the council voted 6-1, with Janet Diaz opposed, to approve a community benefits agreement with Chirisa, over the objections of most of the more than a dozen speakers. It guarantees $20 million, secured by a letter of credit and split between economic development and sustainable development funds; noise no greater than today's at nearby homes and parks; the site's 176 backup diesel generators meeting state and federal air standards and tested only on weekday daytimes; and 100 percent renewable electricity, including nuclear, for the first ten years or millions of dollars in penalties, with operations barred below 60 percent. In return, neither site will be subject to the city's proposed new data center rules if development plans are filed within two years, though the Harrisburg Pike site and Greenfield's second phase still need planning commission approval. "If they meet the requirements of the law, they're going to build the data centers, and you won't have any of the benefits built into this agreement," City Solicitor Barry Handwerger told council. Resident Darrell Lagace called the clean-energy clause "pay to pollute."

Phase two at Greenfield Road, a two-story building of about 735,128 square feet costing up to $2.5 billion including equipment, has gone to the planning commission, according to LNP reporting compiled by a local civic site; the developers say it includes a $200 million PPL substation and switchyard and that each site will use no more than 20,000 gallons of water a day, with air-cooled chillers. In early 2026 the city was seating a five-member Innovation Hub Committee, with a Chirisa or CoreWeave representative among its members, to oversee the $10 million economic development fund. At the state level, the Pennsylvania House voted 104-95 in March 2026 for a bill requiring data centers to pay for grid upgrades and new generation and to meet rising clean-energy shares. What comes next: planning commission review of phase two and of any Harrisburg Pike plans; the phase one building is due for completion in summer 2027.

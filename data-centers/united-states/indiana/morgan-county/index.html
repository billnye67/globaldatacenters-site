Data centers in Morgan County, Indiana: 1 project mapped

1 project tracked · 1 proposed or being built ·
No power figure reported for active projects

Morgan County's data center is Google's, though for most of 2025 nobody in Monrovia was allowed to know that. The project began as "Project Louie": at a community meeting on February 10, 2025, the county plan commission presented a rezoning request that the Morgan County Economic Development Corporation had filed for 391 acres on West Keller Hill Road in Monroe Township, just east of Monrovia, on behalf of an unnamed company. The commissioners approved it unanimously on February 18. In May they approved a development agreement under which, the Indianapolis Business Journal reported, the developer pays no local tax on qualified computing equipment for 40 years, on top of a 10-year, 50% abatement on property tax for the data center buildings, with state tax breaks from the Indiana Economic Development Corp. worth up to $42 million over 35 years. The commissioners had signed a non-disclosure agreement keeping the owner's name private. Google confirmed it was the developer in October 2025, a month after it withdrew a data center plan in Franklin Township in Indianapolis in the face of local opposition, and that same month the commissioners rezoned another 158 acres, bringing the site to 549 acres between Keller Hill Road, North Antioch Road, State Road 42 and West Union Church Road.

Google has promised to spend at least $1 billion and to hire 50 people per data center building; current plans call for four. It has not said publicly how much power or water the campus will use, but filings described by the Business Journal show AES Indiana planning a 1,200MW substation on the site, and documents in a case before the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission put water use at about 4 million gallons a day. Run near that capacity for a year, the paper calculated, the campus would use nearly twice the electricity of all AES Indiana's residential customers. Construction began in November 2025. "Nobody here wants to live across the street from an industrial project like this, and yet you are asking me and my neighbors to carry that burden," Sean Walker, who lives across Antioch Road, told the commissioners before their October vote. Gina and Darin Madsen, who bought their Monrovia house two dozen years ago, told the Arnolt Center for Investigative Journalism they had little time to react; "It was already set in stone," Darin Madsen said. Critics have raised energy and water use, noise and property values, and some have sued the county, Indiana Public Media reported.

The site has also become a fight between the county and the town next to it. In March 2026 the Monrovia town council voted 3-2 to begin annexing 890 acres east of town, including the whole Google campus, which would bring the town tax revenue. The county answered by transferring about 600 acres of Google's property to its redevelopment commission under a 10-year lease-back, which under state law cannot be annexed without the county's consent, and in July the redevelopment commission voted unanimously to make the site part of a tax increment financing district. A Google email leaked in late June offered to pay $12 million to double Monrovia's wastewater plant capacity and upgrade its sewers, "contingent upon the town agreeing to end its current annexation process," with a July 3 deadline. "We are being heavily pressured not to annex," council president Tammy Everett told the Morgan County Correspondent.

The power line is what comes next. On September 14, 2026, AES Indiana chose its route for a 10-mile, 345-kilovolt line from a new Antioch substation near Monrovia to a new Blue Bluff substation at the Eagle Valley plant in Martinsville, roughly following Ind. 39 along the edge of Bradford Woods, on towers of 115 to 195 feet and a 200-foot easement. AES says the line is not a dedicated connection for one customer and that Google will pay all of the incremental generation and transmission cost. But a utility analyst for the state's Office of Utility Consumer Counselor testified to the IURC that the project "would not be needed" if no data centers were built in AES territory. AES has not ruled out eminent domain, plans to start construction in fall 2028 and wants the line in service by fall 2029. Autumn Sojka of the Save Morgan County group said her next aim is to see the officials who backed it voted out.

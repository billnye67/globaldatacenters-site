Data centers in Alabama: 12 projects mapped

12 projects tracked · 8 proposed or being built · 4 operating ·
3,228 MW from the 8 active projects that report a figure
· 8 moratoriums and bans in effect

Alabama's largest proposal is QTS's Project Marvel in Bessemer, southwest of Birmingham, planned as 17 buildings each the size of a Walmart Supercenter. Officials approved the rezoning, and neighbors expect up to a decade of construction. Alabama Power has a power contract for a planned 300-megawatt data center in Birmingham; Beacon Data Centers proposes a $6 billion campus on about 650 acres in Calvert; and residents in Prichard have pushed back on a project tied to Edged. In Lowndes County, Cloverleaf Infrastructure's Project Red Clay would put four 720,000-square-foot buildings along Highway 80 and has requested 1,500 megawatts of power from Alabama Power.

Much of this has happened out of view. Bessemer's mayor and city attorney signed non-disclosure agreements with QTS, and residents of Westover in Shelby County found a county probate record showing a 14-building data center plan after town officials said none was in the works. "If our public officials weren't doing anything wrong, why has it all been done in secret?" said Westover resident Shannon Vanden Heuvel.

Read more

Local governments have started to pause: Homewood and Cullman adopted moratoriums on June 22, Leeds in June, and Springville a one-year moratorium on July 6. Morgan County approved a one-year moratorium on June 23 aimed at a proposed bitcoin mine in Somerville.

A new state law, SB 270 (Act 610 of 2026), takes effect October 1. It requires the Public Service Commission to consider whether Alabama Power's contracts with data centers needing 150 megawatts or more cover their full cost and benefit other customers. The commission's rules for those reviews are still being written; Alabama Power has told it that projects with local approval already should not face the new process. Democratic governor nominee Doug Jones has proposed a one-year statewide moratorium, while Republican nominee Tommy Tuberville has said "we're going to do data centers"; voters decide in November.

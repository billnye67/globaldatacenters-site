Data centers in North Dakota: 8 projects mapped

8 projects tracked · 4 proposed or being built · 4 operating ·
2,541 MW from the 7 active projects that report a figure
· 3 moratoriums and bans in effect

Most of North Dakota's data center building is by one company, Applied Digital. It runs a 106-megawatt center in Jamestown, has put about $5 billion into its Ellendale campus in Dickey County, where about 1,900 construction workers were on site in May, and is building a $3 billion, 280-megawatt AI campus at Harwood outside Fargo. It has also proposed a fourth site in Oliver County, where executive Nick Phillips said it has met its strongest opposition.

In Mandan, Stark Power plans a data center and gas-fired power plant north of Interstate 94, a short distance from Red Trail Elementary, Mandan Middle School and Mandan High School. The company has not applied yet; it is waiting for Mandan and Morton County to finish writing data center rules. "I feel that sometimes it's hard to stop a train, but I think that we have to not quit trying to stop the train," said neighbor Terry Mastel. Stark Power says the project would bring about 150 full-time jobs.

Read more

Dunn County commissioners passed a 12-month moratorium in March on data centers, solar farms and battery storage so staff can write permit rules. NextEra has proposed a data center in neighboring Mercer County, and Critical Data House one at Williston. The state's sales tax break on data center equipment has grown from $5.4 million in 2021 to more than $65 million in 2025, Tax Commissioner Brian Kroshus told lawmakers.

At a special session in September, the Senate rejected a bill to ban secrecy agreements between data center developers and local officials, and a proposal to stop local data center permits through June 2027 did not advance. Majority lawmakers say they will take up data centers when the Legislature meets in January.

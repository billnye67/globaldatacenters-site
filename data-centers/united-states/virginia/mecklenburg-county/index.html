Data centers in Mecklenburg County, Virginia: 2 projects mapped

2 projects tracked · 1 proposed or being built · 1 operating ·
No power figure reported for active projects

Mecklenburg County is where Virginia's data center tax break began. A 2008 bill from Del. Tommy Wright drew Microsoft to this rural Southside county two years later; its Boydton campus, built for nearly $500 million, has been expanded repeatedly since. Microsoft has since built or proposed data centers across Boydton, South Hill, Chase City and Clarksville, on about 3,762 acres, though only two of those sites are on this record so far.

The tax break has paid off for county coffers. Data center revenue now covers about a quarter of the county budget and paid for a new high school. The county's proposed $230 million budget for the fiscal year that began July 1 would cut the real estate rate from 36 cents to 31 cents per $100 and cut the vehicle tax to its lowest since 1999. County Administrator Alex Gottschalk estimates data centers supply 35 to 40 percent of general fund revenue directly. "We picked vehicles because we want to allow everybody to accrue benefits from our prosperity and the data centers," he said.

Read more

The growth has environmental costs. At its Clarksville site, Microsoft self-reported four water violations between October 2023 and March 2024 after a subcontractor cleared land outside its permit, destroying 2.33 acres of forested wetland, and paid a $41,766.90 fine. Dominion Energy was building or had approved three new transmission lines across the county to serve the load as of 2024, with a fourth planned. At a Sierra Club forum in Chase City in September 2024, the group's Paige Wesselink said decisions had been made in "behind the door" conversations between Microsoft and supervisors.

In June 2026, Virginia's legislature and governor settled a budget standoff over data center tax breaks by agreeing to a new tax on data centers' energy use.

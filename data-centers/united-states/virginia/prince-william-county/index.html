Data centers in Prince William County, Virginia: 20 projects mapped

20 projects tracked · 8 proposed or being built · 8 operating · 2 stopped or paused · 2 with no stage on record ·
2,016 MW from the 7 active projects that report a figure

On July 2, 2026 two companies called GW Acquisition Co. filed a notice withdrawing their petition to the Virginia Supreme Court, and with it the Prince William Digital Gateway died after five years. At full build it would have been the largest data center campus in the world: more than 22 million square feet across roughly 2,000 acres along Pageland Lane, running up against the boundary of Manassas National Battlefield Park.

It was beaten in court on process rather than on planning. The Board of County Supervisors approved the rezoning in December 2023 after a public hearing that ran 27 hours. In January 2024 the American Battlefield Trust and a group of residents sued, arguing the county had rushed the zoning and cut short the public's chance to be heard. In August 2025 Prince William Circuit Court Judge Kimberly Irving voided the rezonings over improper public notice, and in March 2026 the Virginia Court of Appeals upheld her. By April the county itself and the co-developer, Compass Datacenters, had both dropped out of the appeal, leaving QTS alone in it until July.

Read more

The county spent public money on the losing side. Supervisors put more than $1.7 million of taxpayer funds into defending the rezoning alongside the developers, including a further $400,000 approved as the case went on. The campaign against it ran to two lawsuits and two electoral recalls.

The appetite changed with the verdict. Less than a week after QTS walked away, the same board rejected a rezoning for Dulles Cloud South, another data center proposal on a site of about 2,000 acres. A county that had been the second-largest data center market in Northern Virginia after Loudoun had turned down two of the biggest schemes ever put to it inside a fortnight.

What happens next is a state question as much as a local one. Virginia's 2026 legislation now requires customers drawing 25 megawatts or more to carry the cost of the generating capacity built for them, and Governor Spanberger's Executive Order 22, signed on September 18, 2026, requires local approval for projects above that threshold and bans the non-disclosure agreements that kept deals like this one out of public view while they were being negotiated.

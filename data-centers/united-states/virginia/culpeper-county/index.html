Data centers in Culpeper County, Virginia: 6 projects mapped

6 projects tracked · 5 proposed or being built · 1 operating ·
1,503 MW from the 4 active projects that report a figure

Culpeper County, southwest of Loudoun County, has approved a string of data center campuses in a few years, most in a county-designated Technology Zone beside the town of Culpeper. The county told WJLA in July 2026 that two data centers were operating, with several more approved or under construction, plus an Amazon campus outside the zone. Growth on that scale has brought organized opposition, a lawsuit, and now a fight over the transmission lines and substation needed to power the industry.

Amazon, through its Marvell Developments subsidiary, rezoned 243 acres on Germanna Highway in Stevensburg, a former horse farm, for a two-building campus of up to 430,000 square feet. Supervisors approved it 4-3 in April 2022 despite the planning commission's recommendation to deny it, and neighboring landowners sued, arguing the vote was illegal spot zoning. "Now it will be like living in a Wal-Mart parking lot," resident Ralph Pritt said at the time. DCD reported AWS planned to invest about $500 million; the project is now under construction.

Read more

In the Technology Zone, supervisors rezoned 121 acres on Nalles Mill Road in December 2023 for Cielo Digital Infrastructure's three buildings, two of 96 megawatts and one of 48; EdgeCore later bought the project, now under construction as EdgeCore Culpeper. In July 2024 they rezoned 69 acres on Nalles Mill and Keyser roads, 4-3, for Red Ace Capital Management's two four-story buildings; Supervisor Susan Gugino said it would be the last such project she'd approve because the zone was full. A 426-acre proposal still filed under the placeholder name XX Tech Park, developer unnamed, seeks 900 megawatts and has been pending since 2024.

Not every proposal has gone through. Two data center bids in Brandy Station, near Culpeper Battlefields State Park, drew resistance; after the planning commission recommended denial, the applicant withdrew. Town residents formed the Coalition to Save Culpeper, and member Andrew Dowdy told the Virginia Mercury that officials had largely ignored residents' concerns.

Power is now the central fight. Dominion Energy and its Valley Link partners have proposed a 115-mile high-voltage line ending at a substation on 88 acres of forest in Richardsville. At a May 19, 2026 meeting, most supervisors made clear the county did not want the substation, and a Dominion representative said, "We do not have an alternative." Dominion says it will file with the State Corporation Commission this fall regardless of the county's position. The county cites a study projecting $2.2 billion in data center tax revenue over 15 years, but unlike Loudoun and Prince William counties, it has no special tax on data center computer equipment.

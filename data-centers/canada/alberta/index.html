Data centers in Alberta: 3 projects mapped

3 projects tracked · 1 proposed or being built · 1 stopped or paused · 1 with no stage on record ·
1,000 MW from the one active project that reports a figure
· 1 moratorium or ban in effect

Alberta is where Canada's biggest data centers are being planned, and the province is courting them. The largest announced so far is Meta's $13 billion, one-gigawatt data center in Sturgeon County, north of Edmonton, announced July 8, 2026 and powered by a new gas plant from 2030. Near Grande Prairie, Kevin O'Leary's proposed $70 billion, 7.5-gigawatt Wonder Valley park got a provincial industrial zone designation on August 26 but still needs its permits. From December 31, grid-connected data centers of 75 megawatts or more pay a 2% levy on their computer hardware.

Sturgeon County had made data centers a permitted use in 2025, so no public vote was needed; hundreds protested in Morinville on August 2. "We don't want this data centre here, and we don't want this data centre anywhere else," said organizer Ben Baril. Greenview's council allowed data centers after a six-minute hearing in November 2024, weeks before Wonder Valley was announced. The Sturgeon Lake Cree Nation is suing the province over a water license for the project; a judge refused in August to throw the case out.

Read more

Other places have said no. On August 17 the Alberta Utilities Commission denied Synapse's 1.4-gigawatt gas plant for a 10-building campus in Olds, about 30 meters from homes: "The site is simply not suitable for the proposed development." Rocky View County, next to Calgary, rejected a 448-hectare campus near Balzac 6-1 in September 2025 and in July 2026 paused new data center rezoning applications until it adopts rules, expected this fall. It has approved zoning for a Beacon AI Centers project near Indus.

The grid is the limit. The province's grid operator capped new large loads at 1,200 megawatts until 2028 after 29 projects asked for more than 16 gigawatts, and the province is favoring developers that bring their own power. At a packed town hall in Lacombe on August 19, Technology Minister Nate Glubish was booed and promised setback rules to keep data centers away from homes. "It's delightful to hear those words but absolutely useless after all these months," said Olds opponent Bek MacIntosh.
